WHAT'S ON THE SCREEN

  1. 1
    The sweep arm One lap every six seconds. Each airplane only moves as the beam crosses it, then holds still — just as a real radar scope behaves.
  2. 2
    An airplane, and its tag The silhouette matches the real type and points the way it is flying. Beside it: callsign, type, altitude in feet — cyan level or climbing, magenta descending.
  3. 3
    Range rings Miles from this building. Currently 10 / 20 / 30.
  4. 4
    Towns underneath A dim map of nearby places, so the picture lines up with geography you know.
  5. 5
    Compass North is up, whatever wall it faces.
  6. 6
    Marks on the rim Traffic beyond the outer ring, parked at the edge on its correct bearing. Zoom out and it comes onto the scope properly.

HOW TO DRIVE IT — TOUCH ONLY, NO BUTTONS AND NO KNOB

TAP AN AIRPLANE

Who is that?

Callsign, airline, where it came from and where it is going, aircraft type, altitude and speed. Swipe down or right to come back to the radar.

PRESS & HOLD ~3½ SECONDS

Only if the Wi-Fi changes

Opens Wi-Fi setup on the screen: tap the new network, type the password. Harmless by accident — it gives up after five minutes and reconnects on its own.

LAST RESORT

Blank, frozen, or plainly sulking?

Unplug the USB-C cable, count to five, plug it back in. Nothing is lost by restarting it, and it is back within a minute.

HOW IT WORKS — TRANSPONDER TO DESK IN A COUPLE OF SECONDS

NEW DATA every ~2 s SWEEP one lap per 6 s SCREEN ~30 frames per second CAPACITY 64 aircraft, nearest first SETTINGS http://flightscnr.local/

Why does it sweep like that?

It knows where everything is, but withholds each update until the arm reaches that bearing.

It went to the clock by itself

The sky is empty. It returns when traffic appears. Screens also drift back after ten seconds.

Why is that airplane so low?

Altitude is above sea level. The ground here is already ~5,000 ft, so a light airplane reads 6,000-something.

Is it watching us?

No camera, no microphone — just a public list of aircraft positions. Please leave it plugged in.
